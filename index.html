Selected Projects

Loco-sheet: Morphing Inchworm Robot Across Rough Terrain

2017–2019 · Soft Robotics · Morphing Locomotion · RoboSoft Competition

Loco-sheet is a flexible sheet-based soft locomotion robot developed to traverse rough and constrained environments, including flat ground, stairs, and low-clearance spaces. The robot uses a tendon-driven flexible PVC sheet body that reversibly morphs between S-shaped and omega-shaped locomotion modes.

Project Overview

The central idea of this project was to use the bending stiffness of a flexible sheet as a functional locomotion element rather than treating the body as a passive structure. By folding and unfolding the sheet with a motor-tendon actuation system, the robot could change its body shape and generate different locomotion behaviors for different terrains.

Locomotion Modes

  • S-shaped locomotion: The robot morphs into an S-shape and uses the bending stiffness of the flexible sheet to lift its center of mass upward, enabling it to climb discrete high terrain such as stairs.
  • Omega-shaped locomotion: The robot unfolds into an omega-like shape and performs two-anchor crawling to move through low-clearance spaces.
  • Turning motion: The robot generates turning behavior by asymmetrically folding and unfolding one side of the sheet while maintaining a folded configuration.

Mechanical Design

  • Designed a morphing body using three layers of 0.5 mm PVC sheets to provide sufficient bending stiffness for lifting the robot's center of mass.
  • Used a motor-tendon actuation system to fold the flexible sheet while allowing elastic unfolding when the tendon was released.
  • Integrated anisotropic friction wheels based on one-way clutches to enable slip-reduced two-anchor crawling in omega-shaped locomotion.
  • Distributed the control board, actuators, and battery along the sheet body to support stable crawling behavior.

My Contributions

  • Designed and fabricated the robot hardware using SOLIDWORKS and laser-cut components.

Results

  • Demonstrated fully untethered locomotion using onboard motors, electronics, and battery.
  • Climbed stairs up to 90 mm in height using S-shaped locomotion.
  • Passed through low-clearance gaps down to 72 mm using omega-shaped locomotion.
  • Achieved flat-ground locomotion speeds of 22 mm/s in S-shaped mode and 18 mm/s in omega-shaped mode.
  • Achieved 2nd place in the RoboSoft 2018 Locomotion Competition.
  • Achieved 1st place in the RoboSoft 2019 Locomotion Competition.
  • Presented at IEEE RoboSoft and selected as a conference finalist.
