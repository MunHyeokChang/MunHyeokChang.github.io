Anthropomorphic Prosthetic Hand for Precision and Tool-Oriented Grasping

2023–Present · Prosthetic Hand · Embedded Actuation · Diagonal Power Grip · Precision Pinch

This project focuses on the design of an anthropomorphic prosthetic hand that supports both tool-oriented diagonal power grasping and precision pinch grasping. The hand is designed to generate diagonal power grip, tip pinch, and lateral pinch while maintaining a compact prosthetic-hand form factor with embedded actuation.

Project Overview

The central goal of this project is to develop a prosthetic hand that can perform both powerful rod-shaped tool grasping and precise manipulation tasks. To achieve this, the hand integrates an optimized active palm mechanism, a passive thumb–finger actuation-order control mechanism, differential tendon routing, and compact embedded actuator modules.

Active Palm-Joint Design

  • Developed a geometric design method to optimize the active palm-joint locations for improving tool-oriented diagonal power grip stability.
  • Defined a grasping quality index based on the object escape opening during diagonal power grip.
  • Analyzed how palm-joint location affects object enclosure and resistance to radial pull-out disturbance.
  • Selected optimized palm-joint locations for the ring and little fingers while preserving anthropomorphic hand morphology.
  • Validated the design through pull-out experiments, showing improved resistance compared with a flexion-only palm-joint configuration.

Passive Actuation-Order Control

  • Proposed a passive thumb–finger actuation-order control mechanism for generating both tip pinch and lateral pinch in a differential tendon-driven hand.
  • Designed a spring–magnet threshold mechanism to regulate the motion onset of the thumb and index finger.
  • Enabled thumb-first motion for tip pinch and finger-first motion for lateral pinch by changing only the thumb abduction position.
  • Performed threshold margin analysis under varying gravity directions to evaluate robustness against hand orientation changes.
  • Verified the intended actuation sequences using threshold tension measurements and Vicon-based motion experiments.

Embedded Actuation and System Integration

  • Integrated differential tendon routing, embedded actuator modules, wrist connector, and onboard control hardware into a complete prosthetic hand prototype.
  • Designed dual-motor tendon actuation modules to combine fast tendon winding and increased tendon force within a compact hand structure.
  • Arranged the actuation and control components on the dorsal side of the hand to preserve the palm-side region for grasping and object contact.
  • Implemented representative grasp modes including diagonal power grip, tip pinch, and lateral pinch.

My Contributions

  • Designed the prosthetic hand mechanism and overall hand architecture.
  • Developed the active palm-joint optimization method for diagonal power grip.
  • Designed the passive thumb–finger actuation-order control mechanism using spring–magnet thresholds.
  • Designed the tendon routing and embedded actuation modules for differential tendon drive.
  • Built and integrated the prototype with mechanical components, actuator modules, and control hardware.
  • Conducted modeling, prototyping, pull-out tests, motion capture experiments, fingertip force measurements, and functional demonstrations.

Technical Skills

Prosthetic Hand Design Anthropomorphic Robotic Hand Tendon-driven Mechanism Underactuated Mechanism Differential Actuation Active Palm Joint Spring–Magnet Threshold Mechanism Embedded Actuation SOLIDWORKS MATLAB Motion Capture Fingertip Force Measurement Functional Demonstration

Results

  • Demonstrated diagonal power grip, tip pinch, and lateral pinch in a single integrated prosthetic hand prototype.
  • Validated active palm-joint design through geometric modeling and pull-out experiments.
  • Confirmed passive switching of thumb–finger actuation order for tip and lateral pinch.
  • Achieved full grasp closure within approximately 1.5 s in the tested configuration.
  • Generated index fingertip force up to approximately 13 N in the tested configuration.
  • Demonstrated representative tool-use and manipulation tasks, including hammering, holding a frying pan, picking and transferring a nail, and holding a card.

Posture-Dependent Variable Transmission Mechanism for Prosthetic Hands

2022–2024 · Prosthetic Finger · Variable Transmission · Tendon-driven Actuation · ISR 2024

This project developed a posture-dependent variable transmission mechanism for tendon-driven prosthetic fingers. The mechanism was designed to reproduce a human-like grasping strategy: fast and gentle motion before object contact, followed by stronger force application after contact.

Project Overview

Prosthetic hands must generate both fast grasping motion and high grip force within strict size and weight constraints. The proposed posture-dependent variable transmission (PDVT) addresses this speed–force trade-off using a spiral-shaped spool and a series elastic compression spring. The mechanism changes the effective transmission ratio according to the finger posture and object contact state.

Mechanism Design

  • Designed a spiral-shaped spool with a continuously decreasing radius for tendon winding.
  • Used a larger effective spool radius before contact to generate faster finger flexion.
  • Used a smaller effective spool radius after contact to increase tendon tension and fingertip force.
  • Integrated a series elastic compression spring to allow additional tendon winding after object contact.
  • Designed the PDVT finger as a tendon-driven underactuated prosthetic finger for adaptive grasping.

Modeling and Parametric Study

  • Modeled the relationship between finger posture, tendon winding length, spool radius, motor rotation, and maximum tendon tension.
  • Conducted parametric studies on spiral spool geometry, including radius ratio and spiral curve length.
  • Conducted parametric studies on compression spring stiffness and allowable compression length.
  • Analyzed the trade-off between grasping speed and tendon force according to spool and spring parameters.

Fabrication

  • Fabricated the complex spiral-shaped spool using a combination of 3D printing and metal components.
  • Used metal pins and bolts in high-load regions to improve mechanical robustness.
  • Fabricated a PDVT-equipped prosthetic finger using 3D printing and metal processing.
  • Built a 70 g PDVT-equipped finger including the motor and transmission components.

My Contributions

  • Proposed and developed the posture-dependent variable transmission concept for prosthetic fingers.
  • Designed the spiral-shaped spool and compression-spring-based tendon transmission.
  • Modeled the speed–force behavior of the PDVT mechanism according to finger posture.
  • Conducted spool and spring parametric studies to determine suitable design parameters.
  • Fabricated the PDVT-equipped prosthetic finger and experimental setup.
  • Evaluated grasping speed, fingertip force, and durability.

Technical Skills

Variable Transmission Tendon-driven Mechanism Underactuated Finger Spiral Spool Design Series Elastic Spring Mechanical Modeling Parametric Study SOLIDWORKS 3D Printing Metal Components Motor Encoder Load Cell Measurement Fingertip Force Test

Results

  • Demonstrated posture-dependent switching between fast motion and high-force grasping.
  • Achieved up to 3.7× faster grasping speed compared with a conventional rigid small-spool mechanism.
  • Achieved up to 5× stronger grip force compared with a conventional rigid large-spool mechanism.
  • Demonstrated fingertip forces up to approximately 2.5 kgf depending on the PDVT design case.
  • Validated PDVT durability through repeated cycling experiments until tendon failure, without structural degradation of the spiral spool.

High-Power Prosthetic Hand with Enhanced Durability

2020–2025 · Prosthetic Hand · High-Power Actuation · Durability Test · Tendon-driven Mechanism

This project focused on developing a human-hand-sized prosthetic hand system capable of fast grasping and high fingertip force while maintaining long-term tendon-driven actuation durability. The project was motivated by the need to integrate high-power actuation into the limited space of an anthropomorphic prosthetic hand.

Project Overview

The goal of this project was to design a prosthetic hand that could achieve grasping within approximately one second and generate fingertip force greater than 45 N within the form factor of a median-sized human hand. The design combined high-power motors, tendon routing, moving-pulley differential actuation, FEM-based structural analysis, and repeated durability testing.

Mechanical and Actuation Design

  • Designed a high-power prosthetic hand targeting fast grasping and fingertip force greater than 45 N.
  • Developed tendon routing to integrate high-power motors with a moving-pulley differential actuation mechanism.
  • Designed a moving-pulley mechanism to distribute actuation force across multiple fingers.
  • Developed a tension-maintaining mechanism for stable tendon-driven grasping.
  • Considered compact packaging constraints to fit the actuation system within a human-hand-sized prosthetic hand.

FEM Analysis and Durability Evaluation

  • Conducted FEM simulations to evaluate the strength and reliability of moving-pulley components under high tendon loads.
  • Selected tendon materials suitable for long-term repeated actuation.
  • Designed an experimental setup for repeated actuation durability testing.
  • Conducted long-term actuation experiments to evaluate mechanical durability of the tendon-driven system.

My Contributions

  • Proposed the original hardware concept for the high-power prosthetic hand system.
  • Designed the tendon-driven actuator structure and moving-pulley differential mechanism.
  • Designed the tendon routing and tension-maintaining mechanism.
  • Performed FEM analysis of high-load mechanical components.
  • Built prototypes and experimental setups for durability validation.
  • Conducted repeated actuation experiments and analyzed system durability.

Technical Skills

Prosthetic Hand Design High-Power Actuation Tendon-driven Mechanism Moving-Pulley Differential Tension Maintaining Mechanism SOLIDWORKS SOLIDWORKS FEM Motor Control Durability Test Experimental Setup Design Force Measurement

Results

  • Designed a human-hand-sized prosthetic hand targeting grasping within 1 second.
  • Designed the hand to achieve fingertip force greater than 45 N.

  • Selected tendon materials and designed a durability experiment setup for long-term actuation.

Anthropomorphic Prosthetic Hand for Sports Swing Activities

2017–2021 · Prosthetic Hand · Articulated Palm · Tendon Routing · TMECH

This project developed an anthropomorphic prosthetic hand designed to achieve a stable diagonal power squeeze grip for sports swing activities. In this project page, the focus is limited to the hand mechanism, including articulated palm design, tendon routing, differential actuation, and grasp validation.

Project Overview

Sports swing activities require a stable diagonal power grip that can resist large external forces and impacts. The proposed prosthetic hand was inspired by anatomical features of the human hand, including the concavity of the palm produced by the fourth and fifth CMC joints, MCP abduction/adduction, and FDP/FDS tendon routing.

Hand Mechanism Design

  • Designed an anthropomorphic prosthetic hand for stable diagonal power squeeze grip.
  • Developed articulated fourth and fifth CMC joints to reproduce palm concavity through flexion and supination.
  • Designed 2-DOF MCP joints to help the fingers conform to diagonally placed cylindrical objects.
  • Mimicked deep transverse metacarpal ligaments using high-stiffness synthetic fiber ropes to restrain excessive palm motion.
  • Designed FDP/FDS-inspired tendon routing to improve finger adaptation and resistance to external forces.

Actuation System

  • Implemented differential tendon-driven actuation to drive multiple finger tendons with fewer actuators.
  • Designed moving-pulley-based differential mechanisms for FDP and FDS tendon actuation.
  • Integrated motor units, encoders, tendon spools, and electronic control components into the hand system.
  • Used tendon routing to allow adaptive finger motion and force distribution during grasping.

My Contributions

  • Designed the prosthetic hand mechanism for diagonal power squeeze grip.
  • Developed the articulated fourth and fifth CMC joints and 2-DOF MCP joint structures.
  • Designed FDP/FDS-inspired tendon routing for adaptive and impact-resilient grasping.
  • Implemented differential tendon-driven actuation for multi-finger grasping with fewer actuators.
  • Fabricated and integrated the hand mechanism, tendon routing, motor units, embedded electronics, and button-based operation.
  • Conducted pressure distribution and impact-resistance experiments to evaluate grasp performance.

Technical Skills

Anthropomorphic Hand Design Prosthetic Hand Articulated Palm CMC Joint Design MCP Joint Design FDP/FDS Tendon Routing Differential Actuation Tendon-driven Mechanism Embedded Electronics Pressure Measurement Impact Test Motion Capture

Results

  • Demonstrated diagonal power squeeze grip using an anthropomorphic prosthetic hand mechanism.
  • Showed that the articulated palm increased ulnar-side finger participation compared with a non-articulated palm design.
  • Improved pressure distribution during diagonal grasping by enabling the ring and little fingers to participate more effectively.
  • Demonstrated improved grasp resilience against external impacts compared with a non-articulated palm design.
  • Validated the hand mechanism through pressure distribution experiments and impact-resistance tests.

Loco-sheet: Morphing Inchworm Robot Across Rough Terrain

2017–2019 · Soft Robotics · Morphing Locomotion · RoboSoft Competition

Loco-sheet is a flexible sheet-based soft locomotion robot developed to traverse rough and constrained environments, including flat ground, stairs, and low-clearance spaces. The robot uses a tendon-driven flexible PVC sheet body that reversibly morphs between S-shaped and omega-shaped locomotion modes.

Project Overview

The central idea of this project was to use the bending stiffness of a flexible sheet as a functional locomotion element rather than treating the body as a passive structure. By folding and unfolding the sheet with a motor-tendon actuation system, the robot could change its body shape and generate different locomotion behaviors for different terrains.

Locomotion Modes

  • S-shaped locomotion: The robot morphs into an S-shape and uses the bending stiffness of the flexible sheet to lift its center of mass upward, enabling it to climb discrete high terrain such as stairs.
  • Omega-shaped locomotion: The robot unfolds into an omega-like shape and performs two-anchor crawling to move through low-clearance spaces.
  • Turning motion: The robot generates turning behavior by asymmetrically folding and unfolding one side of the sheet while maintaining a folded configuration.

Mechanical Design

  • Designed a morphing body using three layers of 0.5 mm PVC sheets to provide sufficient bending stiffness for lifting the robot's center of mass.
  • Used a motor-tendon actuation system to fold the flexible sheet while allowing elastic unfolding when the tendon was released.
  • Integrated anisotropic friction wheels based on one-way clutches to enable slip-reduced two-anchor crawling in omega-shaped locomotion.
  • Distributed the control board, actuators, and battery along the sheet body to support stable crawling behavior.

My Contributions

  • Proposed the main hardware concept of the flexible sheet-based locomotion robot body.
  • Designed and fabricated the robot hardware using SOLIDWORKS and laser-cut components.
  • Implemented the tendon-driven actuation mechanism for sheet folding and unfolding.
  • Designed and fabricated the PCB for the robot system.
  • Implemented Arduino-based motor position control and wireless control.
  • Participated in system integration, testing, and RoboSoft competition preparation.

Technical Skills

Results

  • Demonstrated fully untethered locomotion using onboard motors, electronics, and battery.
  • Climbed stairs up to 90 mm in height using S-shaped locomotion.
  • Passed through low-clearance gaps down to 72 mm using omega-shaped locomotion.
  • Achieved flat-ground locomotion speeds of 22 mm/s in S-shaped mode and 18 mm/s in omega-shaped mode.
  • Achieved 2nd place in the RoboSoft 2018 Locomotion Competition.
  • Achieved 1st place in the RoboSoft 2019 Locomotion Competition.
  • Presented at IEEE RoboSoft and selected as a conference finalist.
