Loco-sheet: Morphing Inchworm Robot Across Rough Terrain

2017–2019 · Soft Robotics · Morphing Locomotion · RoboSoft Competition

Loco-sheet is a flexible sheet-based soft locomotion robot developed to traverse rough and constrained environments, including flat ground, stairs, and low-clearance spaces. The robot uses a tendon-driven flexible PVC sheet body that reversibly morphs between S-shaped and omega-shaped locomotion modes.

Project Overview

The central idea of this project was to use the bending stiffness of a flexible sheet as a functional locomotion element rather than treating the body as a passive structure. By folding and unfolding the sheet with a motor-tendon actuation system, the robot could change its body shape and generate different locomotion behaviors for different terrains.

Locomotion Modes

  • S-shaped locomotion: The robot morphs into an S-shape and uses the bending stiffness of the flexible sheet to lift its center of mass upward, enabling it to climb discrete high terrain such as stairs.
  • Omega-shaped locomotion: The robot unfolds into an omega-like shape and performs two-anchor crawling to move through low-clearance spaces.
  • Turning motion: The robot generates turning behavior by asymmetrically folding and unfolding one side of the sheet while maintaining a folded configuration.

Mechanical Design

  • Designed a morphing body using three layers of 0.5 mm PVC sheets to provide sufficient bending stiffness for lifting the robot's center of mass.
  • Used a motor-tendon actuation system to fold the flexible sheet while allowing elastic unfolding when the tendon was released.
  • Integrated anisotropic friction wheels based on one-way clutches to enable slip-reduced two-anchor crawling in omega-shaped locomotion.
  • Distributed the control board, actuators, and battery along the sheet body to support stable crawling behavior.

My Contributions

  • Proposed the main hardware concept of the flexible sheet-based locomotion robot body.
  • Designed and fabricated the robot hardware using SOLIDWORKS and laser-cut components.
  • Implemented the tendon-driven actuation mechanism for sheet folding and unfolding.
  • Designed and fabricated the PCB for the robot system.
  • Implemented Arduino-based motor position control and wireless control.
  • Participated in system integration, testing, and RoboSoft competition preparation.

Technical Skills

Results

  • Demonstrated fully untethered locomotion using onboard motors, electronics, and battery.
  • Climbed stairs up to 90 mm in height using S-shaped locomotion.
  • Passed through low-clearance gaps down to 72 mm using omega-shaped locomotion.
  • Achieved flat-ground locomotion speeds of 22 mm/s in S-shaped mode and 18 mm/s in omega-shaped mode.
  • Achieved 2nd place in the RoboSoft 2018 Locomotion Competition.
  • Achieved 1st place in the RoboSoft 2019 Locomotion Competition.
  • Presented at IEEE RoboSoft and selected as a conference finalist.

Anthropomorphic Prosthetic Hand for Sports Swing Activities

2017–2021 · Prosthetic Hand · Tendon-driven Mechanism · Power Squeeze Grip

This project developed an anthropomorphic prosthetic hand designed for sports activities that involve high-speed swinging and high-impact object interaction. The hand was designed to hold a golf club diagonally using a stable power squeeze grip, inspired by anatomical features of the human hand.

Project Overview

Conventional prosthetic hands are often designed for activities of daily living, but sports activities such as golf, tennis, baseball, and fishing require stable grasping under high-speed and high-impact conditions. In this project, the hand design focused on reproducing key anatomical functions of the human hand that contribute to efficient swinging: palm concavity, diagonal club grasping, and increased finger stiffness against impact.

Hand Design

  • Articulated palm: Designed 2-DOF CMC joints for the fourth and fifth fingers to reproduce flexion and supination of the metacarpal bones, enabling palm concavity for diagonal club grasping.
  • Power squeeze grip: Enabled the fingers, palm, and thumb to wrap around a diagonally placed club, improving grasp stability during swing impact.
  • FDP/FDS-inspired tendon routing: Designed tendon paths inspired by human finger flexor tendons to actuate finger joints and reinforce grasping against external forces.
  • 2-DOF underactuated finger joints: Designed CMC and MCP joint mechanisms that allow the fingers to adapt to the club surface while maintaining compact mechanical structure.
  • Differential actuation system: Implemented a compact tendon actuation system to drive multiple finger tendons with fewer actuators while allowing adaptive force distribution among fingers.

My Contributions

  • Designed the anthropomorphic prosthetic hand mechanism focused on diagonal power squeeze grip.
  • Developed the articulated palm structure using 2-DOF CMC joints for the fourth and fifth fingers.
  • Designed the tendon routing for FDP/FDS-inspired finger actuation.
  • Implemented the differential actuation mechanism for underactuated finger control.
  • Built and integrated the prosthetic hand prototype for grasping and impact experiments.
  • Contributed to experimental validation of grasp pressure distribution and grasp resilience under impact.

Technical Skills

Results

  • Achieved palm concavity using articulated fourth and fifth CMC joints for diagonal club grasping.
  • Enabled all fingers, including the ring and little fingers, to contribute to power squeeze grip.
  • Increased pressure on the ulnar side of the hand compared with a non-articulated palm design.
  • Improved grasp resilience against impact in palmar, ulnar, and radial directions.
  • Demonstrated stable club holding under high-speed swing and impact conditions.
